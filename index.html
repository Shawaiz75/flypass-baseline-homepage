The part nobody warns you about

That worry is fair. Most Schengen refusals come down to problems that were fixable: a file that does not match the applicant's real circumstances, weak financial proof, or an application sent to the wrong consulate.

Flypass Holidays exists to take that weight off you. Not with promises. With preparation.

The definition

What is a Schengen visa agent, and what does one actually do?

A Schengen visa agent is a private consultant who prepares your short-stay Type C visa application before you submit it. A good agent confirms which consulate must receive your case, assembles and checks your documents, drafts your cover letter and itinerary, and audits the complete file before your appointment. No agent can issue a visa or influence the decision. Only the consulate does that.

That last sentence is the one most people never hear before they pay someone. It is worth reading twice.

Agents are not a legal requirement. You are always free to apply on your own, directly through the consulate of your main destination or its official application centre. What you buy from an agent is preparation, judgement and a second set of eyes on a file you only get to submit once.

The short version

Schengen visa from the UK, in brief

The questions people ask us first, answered before you scroll. Everything below applies to a short-stay Type C visa applied for from the United Kingdom.

Who needs one
Your passport decides, not your UK status. Indefinite leave to remain or a UK visa does not exempt you. If your nationality requires a Schengen visa, you need one as a long-term UK resident.
Where you apply
At the consulate of your main destination — the country where you will spend the most nights. Nights split equally, it is the country you enter first. How the rule works.
Who takes it in
A visa application centre run by VFS Global, TLScontact, BLS International or GVCW, depending on the country. You attend in person to give biometrics.
Consulate fee
EUR 90 for an adult, EUR 45 for a child aged six to eleven. Set by the EU and paid directly to the consulate or application centre — never to us.
Insurance
Travel medical cover of at least EUR 30,000, valid in all Schengen states for the whole of your stay. Mandatory, and checked.
How long you can stay
Up to 90 days in any rolling 180-day period, counted across the Schengen area as a whole rather than per country.
How long it takes
15 calendar days from your appointment as standard — in practice two to three weeks — and up to 45 days where extra checks apply.
When to apply
Applications are accepted from six months before travel. In our experience, appointment availability in peak season is the real bottleneck, so start two to three months out.

What you will need

What documents do I need for a Schengen visa from the UK?

Every consulate publishes its own checklist and they differ in the detail, but a short-stay application from the UK is built from the same ten things almost everywhere. The list is not the hard part. Making the ten agree with each other is.

  1. 01

    Application form

    Completed and signed. For most countries this is filled in on the consulate’s own portal rather than on paper, and the answers on it have to match every document behind it.

  2. 02

    Passport

    Valid for at least three months beyond the date you intend to leave the Schengen area, issued within the previous ten years, with at least two blank pages.

  3. 03

    Two biometric photographs

    Recent, and to ICAO specification. Application centres reject photographs more often than people expect, usually for background, expression or crop.

  4. 04

    Proof of UK immigration status

    Your BRP, eVisa share code or valid UK visa, showing leave that covers your return. This is the document that establishes you may apply from the UK at all.

  5. 05

    Travel medical insurance

    Minimum EUR 30,000 of cover, valid in all Schengen states for the whole of your stay. Not just the country you apply to — all of them.

  6. 06

    Proof of accommodation

    Covering every night of the trip, with no unexplained gaps. Bookings, a host’s invitation, or a mix of both where the trip moves around.

  7. 07

    Travel booking and itinerary

    A return or onward booking and a day-by-day plan that agrees with your accommodation, your dates and your stated purpose.

  8. 08

    Proof of funds

    Usually three to six months of bank statements. This is where most refusals actually begin — not a missing paper, but money that raises a question nobody answered.

  9. 09

    Proof of your position in the UK

    An employer’s letter and recent payslips, a university enrolment letter, or company documents if you are self-employed. What ties you here, and what you are coming back to.

  10. 10

    Cover letter

    Not on any official checklist, and the single most useful page in the file. It explains the trip, connects the documents to each other, and answers the questions the consulate would otherwise have to guess at.

Treat this as the shape of a file, not as the checklist. The consulate handling your application publishes the list that actually governs it, and it can ask for more depending on your nationality, your status and your history. Confirming which consulate that is, and what it wants to see from someone in your position, is step one of our process.
